# Audio Breach — Devlog #002: Bringing the Vision to Life

In the first devlog, I introduced the idea behind **Audio Breach** and the atmosphere I want the game to create. This time, I want to focus on turning that vision into something playable.

## From Idea to Implementation

The main goal is to make the player question what they see and hear. Instead of relying on constant action, Audio Breach aims to build tension through small details, unexpected changes, and the feeling that something isn't quite right.

That means the environment and sound design need to work together. Even a familiar room should be able to feel different when the player notices something unusual.

## Building the Atmosphere

Atmosphere isn't just about darkness or scary sounds. It's about making the player pay attention.

Small changes in the environment, subtle audio cues, and moments of silence can make the player wonder whether something has changed—or whether they simply missed it before.

I'm focusing on making these details feel intentional rather than random. The goal is to make players curious enough to investigate, while still leaving them uncertain about what they might discover.

## What Comes Next

The next step is to keep developing the core experience and experiment with how environmental details and sound can influence the player's perception.

Audio Breach is still a work in progress, but the direction is becoming clearer: a game where paying attention matters, and where even the smallest detail can make you question what is happening.

**Sometimes, the most unsettling thing is noticing that something is different.**
